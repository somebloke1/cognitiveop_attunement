# Temporal Structure of Judgment (Compressed)

## Core Distinction

**Invariant expressions**: Same proposition regardless of when/where uttered ("2+2=4", "Water is H2O")
**Relative expressions**: Different propositions depending on standpoint ("John is here now", "It is cold")

**Critical**: The copula 'is' in abstract expressions uses an **invariant tense** abstracting from particular times.

## The Judge's Temporal Situation

The judge operates within a temporal horizon:
1. Judgment has duration (not instantaneous)
2. The duration of judging runs concurrent with the duration of what is judged
3. "What is 'now' for me writing is not 'now' for you reading"

## Reference Frames

Judgment requires situating within reference frames:
- **Personal**: Moves/turns with the judge, keeps 'now' synchronized with psychological present
- **Public**: Shared standards (clocks, calendars)
- **Special**: Formal coordinate systems

**Key**: The absolute resides not in particular places/times but in abstract propositions whose expression remains invariant under permissible transformations.

## Temporal Structure of the Virtually Unconditioned

A proposition can be:
- Fulfilled at T1 but not T2 (conditions change)
- Unfulfilled at T1 but fulfilled at T2 (evidence emerges)
- Conditionally fulfilled (if temporal conditions obtain)

## Evidence Availability is Temporal

- Evidence "not yet available" at judge's time
- Evidence "was available but now lost"
- Evidence "will become available"

## The Copula in Judgment

When judging "X is true", distinguish:
- **Invariant 'is'**: Abstract laws (timeless validity)
- **Present-tense 'is'**: Particular facts (valid now, for this observer)
- **Tensed 'was/will be'**: Historical/prospective claims

## Evaluation Implications

For temporal judgment evaluation:
1. **Scope-matching**: Does temporal scope of judgment match temporal scope of evidence?
2. **Aspectual awareness**: Does student recognize perfective/imperfective/ongoing status?
3. **Standpoint-relativity**: Does student account for temporal standpoint of judge vs. judged?

Failure modes:
- Treating relative expressions as invariant
- Ignoring evidence currency/availability
- Temporal non-sequiturs (concluding about T2 from evidence about T1)
