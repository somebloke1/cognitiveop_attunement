# Temporal Structure of Judgment

## Source
Lonergan, *Insight*, Chapter 5: "Space and Time"

## Core Insight: The Absolute and the Relative

Lonergan distinguishes between:
- **Invariant expressions**: Stand for the same proposition regardless of when/where uttered (e.g., "2 + 2 = 4", "Pure water is H2O")
- **Relative expressions**: Stand for different propositions depending on spatiotemporal standpoint (e.g., "John is here now", "It is cold")

**Critical point**: The copula 'is' in abstract expressions occurs "not in the ordinary present tense but rather in an **invariant tense** that abstracts from particular times."

## The Judge's Temporal Situation

From Lonergan's analysis of simultaneity:

> "Already we have remarked that we experience duration both in the sense that the experiencing is over time and in the sense that the experienced endures through time."

The judge operates within a temporal horizon:
1. **The watching has duration** - judgment is not instantaneous
2. **The duration of watching runs concurrently with the duration of what is watched**
3. **Simultaneity is grounded in the duration immanent in the flow of consciousness**

This means: "What is 'now' for me writing is not 'now' for you reading."

## Reference Frames for Judgment

Just as spatial/temporal measurement requires reference frames, judgment requires:

1. **Personal reference frame**: "moves when he moves, turns when he turns, and keeps its 'now' synchronized with his psychological present"
2. **Public reference frames**: Shared standards (clocks, calendars, maps)
3. **Special reference frames**: Formal coordinate systems

**Key insight**: "It would be a mistake to look for the fixed or absolute on the level of particular places and times; the only absolute relevant to Space and Time resides in the abstract propositions whose expression remains invariant under permissible transformations of reference frame."

## Implications for Training Judgment

### 1. The Virtually Unconditioned Has Temporal Structure

A proposition can be:
- **Fulfilled at T1 but not T2** (conditions change)
- **Unfulfilled at T1 but fulfilled at T2** (evidence emerges)
- **Conditionally fulfilled** (if certain temporal conditions obtain)

Training must include:
- Retrospective judgments ("Was X true at T?")
- Prospective judgments ("Will X be true if conditions C obtain?")
- Conditional/subjunctive judgments ("Would X have been true if...?")

### 2. Evidence Availability is Temporal

The NX tokens from the external test framework address this:
- Evidence "not yet available" at judge's time
- Evidence that "was available but is now lost"
- Evidence that "will become available"

### 3. Simultaneity is Not Invariant

Just as physical simultaneity is relative to reference frame, evidential simultaneity in judgment is relative to:
- The judge's epistemic position
- The temporal structure of the matter judged
- The conditions' own temporal unfolding

### 4. The Copula in Judgment

When we judge "X is true", we must distinguish:
- **Invariant 'is'**: Abstract laws (timeless validity)
- **Present-tense 'is'**: Particular facts (valid now, for this observer)
- **Tensed 'was/will be'**: Historical/prospective claims

## Concrete vs Abstract Intelligibility

From Lonergan's conclusion:

> "The abstract intelligibility of Space and Time is coincident with the solution of a problem in physics. It is the intelligibility, not so much of Space and Time, as of physical objects in their spatiotemporal relations."

For judgment:
- **Abstract intelligibility**: The invariant structure of the virtually unconditioned
- **Concrete intelligibility**: How that structure is instantiated in particular judgments from particular temporal standpoints

The concrete intelligibility is linked to **emergent probability**:

> "The concrete intelligibility of Time is that it grounds the possibility of successive realizations in accord with probabilities."

## Application to Cognitive Operation Training

### Current Gap
The current architecture assumes a timeless present: "Is X true?" without situating:
- The judge temporally
- The proposition temporally  
- The conditions temporally

### Required Extensions

1. **Temporal markers in training data**:
   - When is the judge making this judgment?
   - When did/does/will the proposition hold?
   - When are/were/will the conditions be fulfilled?

2. **Subjunctive judgment types**:
   - Counterfactuals: "If X had occurred, Y would have been true"
   - Hypotheticals: "If X were to occur, Y would be true"
   - Future contingents: "X will be true if C obtains"

3. **Reference frame transformations**:
   - Same evidence, different temporal standpoints → potentially different judgments
   - This is NOT relativism but recognition of the structure of concrete judgment

### The Sanskrit/Greek/Hebrew Anchors

These provide grammatical resources for temporal aspect that English lacks:
- **Sanskrit**: Rich aspectual system (perfective, imperfective, aorist)
- **Greek**: Aspect as primary, tense as secondary
- **Hebrew**: Aspect-based verbal system (perfect/imperfect)

These are essential scaffolding for training temporal judgment, not decorative vocabulary.

## Quote for Meditation

> "Galileo, Newton, and Kant were looking for some sort of absolute, but they were looking in the wrong places. They sought the real as opposed to the apparent, only to end up with everything apparent, the notion of the real included. Let us follow a different tack. Then every content of experience will be equally valid, for all are equally given, and all equally are to be explained. Next, explanations result from enriching abstraction, and so they are abstract, and their proper expression must be invariant. Thirdly, not every explanation is equally correct; some can be verified, and some cannot. There follows at once the conclusion that the real, objective, true consists of what is known by formulating and verifying invariant principles and laws."

This is the philosophical foundation: the absolute in judgment resides in the invariant structure of grasping the virtually unconditioned, not in any particular temporal standpoint.
