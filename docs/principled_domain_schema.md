# Principled Domain Schema for Judgment Training

A philosophically grounded schema for differentiating domains of judgment, derived from Lonergan's analysis of cognitive operations in *Insight*. This schema replaces arbitrary aggregation with systematic differentiation based on the structure of judgment itself.

## 1. Purpose and Scope

This document provides the theoretical foundation for **generating** test cases for judgment training. It is comprehensive by design—the full schema need not be communicated to evaluators, as the comprehensiveness is **embedded** in the generated cases themselves.

> **Design Principle**: The generation of test cases can and should be methodically comprehensive. The evaluation of test cases need not make all of this explicit. The evaluator needs enough to judge *this case*—not the entire schema that generated it. This is analogous to a well-designed exam that tests understanding without the student knowing the full taxonomy of misconceptions the exam was designed to detect.

## 2. Philosophical Foundations

### 2.1 The Structure of Judgment

Judgment is the cognitive act of grasping the **virtually unconditioned** and thereby moving from hypothesis to affirmation (or denial). The virtually unconditioned has three components:

1. **The Conditioned**: A proposition that is the content of a possible judgment
2. **The Link**: The connection between the conditioned and its conditions ("If X, Y, Z, then the proposition")
3. **The Fulfillment**: The conditions are fulfilled

When the conditions are fulfilled, the conditioned becomes virtually unconditioned—not absolutely unconditioned (it might not have been), but conditionally necessary (given that the conditions are met, it cannot be otherwise).

**The Three Judgment Outcomes**:
- **Yes (Affirmation)**: Conditions are fulfilled. Evidence positively establishes that conditions are met.
- **No (Denial)**: Evidence obliges negation. Sufficient evidence determines that at least one condition is NOT fulfilled.
- **Insufficient**: The data required to judge does not yet exist or is inaccessible. The question cannot be answered because relevant information has not been obtained.

### 2.2 Mode of Fulfillment as Primary Differentiator

The **primary genus** of judgment is determined by **where the virtually unconditioned is grasped**—that is, where fulfillment is found:

| Mode | Where Fulfillment Is Found | Reversion Required? |
|------|---------------------------|---------------------|
| **Formal** | Intelligibility itself (coherence of system) | No—remains within concepts |
| **Empirical** | Sense data via verified correlations | Yes—to measurements, observations |
| **Common Sense** | Pragmatic situation as related to us | Yes—to concrete circumstances |
| **Dialectical** | Performative self-consistency | Yes—to the act of affirming itself |

This differentiation is **not by subject matter** but by the **structure of the cognitive act**. The same subject matter may be approached through different modes (e.g., the history of mathematics can be studied empirically, while mathematical truths are grasped formally).

### 2.3 Heuristic Structures

Each mode operates through characteristic **heuristic structures**—anticipatory frameworks that guide inquiry:

| Heuristic | What It Seeks | Anticipation | Output |
|-----------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| **Classical** | Systematic relations | "Similars are similarly understood" | Universal laws ("always/never") |
| **Statistical** | Intelligibility of the nonsystematic | "The nonsystematic cannot systematically diverge from the systematic" | Ideal frequencies, probabilities |
| **Genetic** | Development over time | Earlier schemes condition later | Operators of development |
| **Dialectical** | Coherence of positions | Positions invite development; counterpositions invite reversal | Identification of positions vs. counterpositions |

## 3. The Primacy of Common Sense

### 3.1 Common Sense as Ground

> **Design Principle**: The schema recognizes that common sense is the ground of human cognition, not merely one domain among others. Methodical intelligence (scientific, philosophical) emerges from common sense through deliberate cultivation and returns to it through institutional structures, formalizations, and protocols. The distribution of test cases need not mirror the statistical distribution of human judgments, but the schema must not inadvertently marginalize common sense by treating specialized modes as primary.

The vast majority of human judgments throughout history are rooted in common sense rather than pure method. Common sense is:
- **Concrete and particular**: Concerned with this situation, not universal laws
- **Incomplete by design**: A nucleus of insights completed by the situation at hand
- **Related to us**: Descriptive (relates things to us) rather than explanatory (relates things to one another)
- **Self-correcting through collaboration**: Social interaction reveals blind spots

### 3.2 Professional Common Sense and Mediating Structures

It takes structures like "a physician's office" and the notion of "an appointment" to bring a person of common sense into a context of **professional common sense**—the physician's practical judgment, informed by but not identical with medical science.

**Professional common sense** is neither:
- Pure explanatory science (which relates symptoms to pathophysiology)
- Nor ordinary common sense (untrained practical intelligence)

It is **common sense organized and ordered by methodical intelligence** through:
- **Training**: Methodically-guided accumulation of insights
- **Protocols**: Formalizations that constrain and direct judgment
- **Institutional structures**: The office, the appointment, the examination
- **Professional culture**: Norms, expectations, habits of the trade

Much of human society consists in the gradual emergence of such formalizations and protocols that allow methodical sources of understanding to organize and order unmethodical common sense.

### 3.3 Varieties of Common Sense

| Variety | Description | How Methodical Sources Enter |
|---------|-------------|------------------------------|
| **Everyday practical** | Cooking, navigation, household | Folk wisdom, trial and error |
| **Social/interpersonal** | Reading situations, predicting behavior | Cultural knowledge, accumulated experience |
| **Professional** | Trades, crafts, applied expertise | Training, protocols, institutional structures |
| **Methodically-informed** | Layperson dealing with expert domains | Popularizations, consultations, interfaces |
| **Generational/cultural** | Proverbs, traditional practices | Transmission across generations |

### 3.4 The Interface Problem

Much of human judgment occurs at the **interface** between common sense and methodical intelligence:
- Patient consulting physician
- Citizen reading science journalism
- Manager making decisions using data analysis
- Voter evaluating policy claims

This interface is the crucial locus where:
- Methodical understanding is communicated to common sense
- Common sense appropriates (or misappropriates) methodical claims
- Pop science emerges when the interface malfunctions

## 4. Modes of Judgment

### 4.1 Formal Mode

**Definition**: Conditions fulfilled in intelligibility itself—no reversion to sense data required.

**Structure**: 
- Proof proceeds from axioms/definitions through valid inference
- Fulfillment is grasped when the proof is seen to be valid
- The content is necessary given the premises

**Varieties**:
- Pure mathematics (number theory, algebra, analysis)
- Formal logic (propositional, predicate)
- Set theory and foundations

**Characteristic Questions**:
- Is this proof valid?
- Does this follow from the axioms?
- Is this definition coherent?

**Pitfalls**:
- Confusing plausibility with proof
- Hidden assumptions
- Incomplete induction (checking cases ≠ proving universally)

### 4.2 Empirical Mode

**Definition**: Conditions fulfilled by reversion to sense data via verified correlations.

**Structure**:
- Hypothesis connected to observable conditions
- Fulfillment found in experimental verification, observation, measurement
- Content is contingent—verified but could have been otherwise

**Higher Integration Principle**: Within the empirical mode, sciences are differentiated by **levels of integration**. Each higher level makes systematic what the lower must regard as coincidental:

| Level | Makes Systematic | Conjugates | Examples |
|-------|------------------|------------|----------|
| **Physical** | Subatomic regularities | Mass, charge, field vectors | Physics, chemistry, astronomy |
| **Biological** | Metabolism, reproduction, organic function | Organic schemes of recurrence | Biology, biochemistry, ecology |
| **Sensitive** | Coordinated behavior, perception | Percepts, images, affects | Animal psychology, neuroscience |
| **Intelligent** | Meaning, culture, history | Insights, judgments, decisions | Human sciences, history, economics |

**Critical Note**: The transition between levels is **not logical**—it requires insight into images based on the lower science. Biology cannot be derived from chemistry; the higher makes systematic what the lower must regard as "mere patterns of happy coincidences."

**Heuristic Structures in Empirical Mode**:
- **Classical**: Seeks systematic laws (physics, chemistry)
- **Statistical**: Seeks ideal frequencies (thermodynamics, genetics)
- **Genetic**: Seeks developmental operators (evolutionary biology, developmental psychology)

### 4.3 Common Sense Mode

**Definition**: Conditions fulfilled in the pragmatic situation as related to us.

**Structure**:
- Incomplete set of insights, completed by scrutiny of the particular situation
- Fulfillment found in "it works"—pragmatic success
- Content is descriptive (relates things to us, not to one another)

**Key Characteristics**:
- Never aspires to universally valid knowledge
- Deals with the concrete and particular
- Self-corrects through social collaboration
- Cannot analyze itself or formulate its own nucleus abstractly

**Varieties**: See Section 3.3 above.

**Pitfalls**:
- **General bias**: Extending legitimate concern for the concrete into disregard of larger issues and indifference to long-term results
- Assuming what works here works everywhere
- Confusing familiarity with understanding

### 4.4 Dialectical Mode

**Definition**: Conditions fulfilled in performative self-consistency.

**Structure**:
- The proposition is assessed by asking: Can this be held coherently by one who grasps it intelligently and affirms it reasonably?
- Fulfillment found by checking whether the act of affirming contradicts the content affirmed
- **Positions**: Coherent with intelligent inquiry → invite development
- **Counterpositions**: Incoherent with the act of affirming them → invite reversal

**The Basic Positions**:
- Real = being (what is intelligently grasped and reasonably affirmed)
- Knowing = intelligent inquiry and critical reflection
- Objectivity = consequence of authentic inquiry

**The Counterpositions** (self-defeating on affirmation):
- Real = "already out there now" (biological extroversion applied to intellectual matters)
- Knowing = "taking a look" (ocular metaphor for what is properly inquiry)
- Objectivity = confrontation that makes the known present to the knower

**Characteristic Questions**:
- Is this position coherent with affirming it?
- Does this claim undermine itself?
- Which interpretation invites development vs. reversal?

## 5. Epistemic Context as Dimension

Epistemic context is **not a domain parallel to the modes** but a **dimension** that can characterize any judgment about expression or communication.

### 5.1 The Structure of Expression

Expression is governed by a practical insight **F** that depends on:
- **A**: The principal insight to be communicated
- **B**: Grasp of the audience's habitual intellectual development
- **D**: Grasp of deficiencies in insight that must be overcome

### 5.2 Cooperative Context

**Definition**: Speaker's practical insight F is oriented toward communicating A accurately.

The interpreter's task is to reconstruct A from the expression, accounting for the differences between audiences (reflective interpretation).

**Assumption**: Good faith—the speaker is trying to be understood.

### 5.3 Adversarial Context

**Definition**: Speaker's practical insight F is oriented toward producing a **false belief** about A in the hearer.

The interpreter's task is to:
1. Detect that expression ≠ A (that the speaker is not communicating in good faith)
2. Reconstruct the actual A (what the speaker actually knows/believes)
3. Identify the deceptive strategy

**Varieties**:
- **Deliberate deception**: Lies, fraud, scams
- **Manipulation**: Emotional manipulation, gaslighting, strategic framing
- **Propaganda**: Institutional messaging designed to persuade rather than inform
- **Strategic ambiguity**: Deliberately vague or deniable statements
- **Adversarial debate**: Arguments designed to win rather than find truth

**Key Pitfall**: Assuming good faith when it is absent.

**Note**: Sarcasm and irony are **cooperative** in intent (the speaker wants to be understood as meaning the opposite) but require recognizing non-literal meaning.

### 5.4 Self-Defeating Context

**Definition**: The position P contradicts the act of affirming P.

This is where **epistemic context meets dialectical mode**—the speaker may or may not be aware that their position is self-defeating.

**Examples**:
- "All knowledge is merely neural activity" (affirmed as knowledge that is more than neural activity)
- "There is no truth" (presented as a truth)
- "You can't trust anything you read" (written to be read and trusted)

## 6. Pathological Forms

### 6.1 The Structure of Bias

Lonergan identifies a hierarchy of bias that distorts judgment:

| Bias | Source | Characteristic Distortion |
|------|--------|---------------------------|
| **Individual (dramatic)** | Flight from self-consciousness | Scotosis—blind spots in self-knowledge |
| **Individual (egoistic)** | Self-interest | Rationalizing self-serving judgments |
| **Group** | Class/national interest | Presenting group advantage as universal principle |
| **General** | Common sense refusing its limits | Disregarding larger issues, indifference to long-term results |

### 6.2 Pop Science as Counterposition

> **Key Insight**: Pop science is the coagulation of a form of common sense **conditioned by the prior emergence of the scientific method**. It appropriates scientific vocabulary and prestige while refusing the detachment and disinterestedness that makes scientific method what it is.

Pop science is **not a domain parallel to physics or biology**. It is a **dialectical counterposition**—the rationalization of general bias using the form without the substance of scientific judgment.

**From Lonergan's "General Bias" section**:
> "[General bias's] rejection of the normative significance of detached and disinterested intelligence makes it radically uncritical. It possesses no standpoint from which it can distinguish between social achievement and the social surd."

**Structure of Pop Science**:
- Conditions are **claimed** but **not fulfilled**
- Scientific vocabulary without understanding
- Authority appeal over evidence
- Form of scientific judgment without substance of verification

**Detection Criteria**:
1. Does the judgment actually revert to what can be verified?
2. Are conditions fulfilled or merely claimed?
3. Is there vocabulary without correlative insight?
4. "The question is NOT how many people say it is obvious, nor how great is their authority and renown, but simply what is the evidence."

### 6.3 The Social Surd

When general bias operates cumulatively, it produces a **social surd**—an increasingly significant residue that:
1. Is immanent in the social facts
2. Is not intelligible
3. Cannot be abstracted from if one is to consider the facts as they are

Pop science is both a symptom of and contributor to the social surd—it fills the gap between common sense and methodical intelligence with pseudo-understanding that blocks genuine appropriation.

## 7. Edge Cases as Probabilistic Modifiers

Edge cases are **not separate domains** but **probabilistic complications** that can arise in any judgment.

> **Implementation Principle**: For any generated case, roll probabilistically for whether to introduce an edge-case complication. If triggered, prompt for details appropriate to the edge-case type and other case factors, ensuring coherent integration. "It takes insight into oversight to train a model to handle oversight."

| Structure | Challenge | Notes |
|-----------|-----------|-------|
| **Self-reference** | Proposition refers to itself or its own affirmation | Especially relevant in dialectical mode |
| **Vagueness (sorites)** | No sharp boundary for predicate application | Demands recognizing borderline cases |
| **Future contingent** | Event has not yet occurred | Integrates with temporal structure |
| **Counterfactual** | No actual data to revert to | Historical, ethical, planning contexts |
| **Category error** | Question presupposes wrong categories | Recognizing malformed questions |

## 8. Design Principles

1. **Systematic, not arbitrary**: Domains derived from structure of judgment, not aggregated from "what we happened upon"

2. **Common sense as ground**: Not one domain among many but the ocean in which islands of methodical intelligence float

3. **Mode of fulfillment as genus**: The primary differentiator is **where the virtually unconditioned is grasped**

4. **Epistemic context as dimension**: Adversarial/cooperative cuts across all modes—not a separate domain

5. **Pathologies as counterpositions**: Pop science and bias are dialectical distortions, not taxonomic categories

6. **Edge cases as modifiers**: Probabilistic complications, not separate domains

7. **Generation ≠ Evaluation**: The full schema guides generation; evaluation needs only what's relevant to the case at hand

8. **Empirical review for coverage**: Cell count to be resolved by examining actual generation output, not fixed a priori
